# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Sim racers on Linux, of every level, with **priority to the beginner**. The beginner bought a wheel and pedals, does not know what a driver is and never wants to open a terminal: the rig must work without reading anything. The enthusiast tunes force feedback, curves and dead zones and knows the vocabulary: everything advanced is there, one step away, but never in the beginner's way.

They use the app in two situations, so both themes matter equally:

- **At the rig**, seated in driving position, often in a dim room, the screen at arm's length or farther, a few minutes before driving: checking that everything responds, switching preset, nudging the rotation.
- **At the desk**, in normal light, mouse and keyboard at hand: first setup, adding a device, careful tuning.

## Product Purpose

SimRig configures a whole sim-racing rig, of any brand and any mix of brands, from one place: wheel base, steering wheel, pedals, shifter, handbrake. It installs the drivers a device needs, shows every component live, lets the user calibrate, tune and save presets, and then gets out of the way: the drivers keep working with the app closed.

Success: a beginner plugs in a new device, opens the app, and within a couple of minutes sees it moving on screen, calibrated and saved, without having understood (or needed to understand) anything about drivers, permissions or Linux.

## Brand Personality

**Sporty, technical, essential.** The language of motorsport instrumentation and telemetry, made clean: values that read at a glance from the seat, precise numbers, nothing decorative. Confident and calm rather than loud. It speaks plainly to the beginner and never talks down to the expert.

The voice is **impersonal and technical**, like instrumentation: "4 devices found", "The password is asked once", never "we found" or "we ask you". No first person, no chatter.

What to keep from manufacturers' apps: **big, readable live values** (angle, pedal pressure, gear) and **presets one click away** from the home.

## Anti-references

- **Aggressive RGB gamer** look: neon, glows, cut corners, fake carbon fibre.
- **Overloaded cockpit**: dozens of numbers at once that intimidate the beginner. Density is earned, not the default.
- **Generic SaaS dashboard**: identical cards, purple accents, giant hero metrics.
- **2000s configuration panel**: walls of tabs, checkboxes and dropdowns.

## Design Principles

1. **Plug in, see it move.** The first proof that things work is the device moving on screen in real time; every flow leads there as fast as possible.
2. **Simple by default, deep on demand.** The beginner's path never shows an advanced setting; the expert reaches every one in a single step. Unsupported items are shown disabled with the reason, never hidden.
3. **Readable from the seat.** Live values and the current state must be legible at a distance and at a glance, in both themes.
4. **The app is a guest.** It never needs root to run, asks for a password only when something touches the system, and leaves nothing running when closed. The interface should feel as light as that promise.
5. **Brand-agnostic by construction.** Nothing in the interface favours a manufacturer; a mixed rig looks as native as a single-brand one.
6. **Say it once, and only when it matters.** Every piece of information appears in one place on a screen, never repeated in a chip or a second label. Status is shown when something needs attention (a driver to install, a restart, an untested model), not to confirm that all is well: no "active", "tested" or "OK" badges.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG 2.2 AA contrast in both themes, including live values over device images.
- State is never conveyed by color alone (color-blind safe): pair color with shape, icon, text or position.
- Reduced motion respected: live device animation stays (it is information), decorative transitions are removed.
- Fully usable with the keyboard, visible focus everywhere.
- Text and labels are translated (English and Italian at first); layouts must tolerate longer strings.
